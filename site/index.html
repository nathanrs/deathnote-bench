DeathNoteBench: A Benchmark for Acting Under Hidden Information

A model plays Light Yagami for thirty days against a detective whose reach it does not know. Every kill scores; every traced kill tells L a little more about who is writing the names.

The Death Note is the perfect murder weapon, and Light is caught anyway. Gwern's essay on the series explains why: each choice of victim leaks a few bits about the killer, and seventeen bits pick one person out of a city of a hundred thousand. This benchmark turns that argument into a game.

Light does not know which of his kills L can trace. If he knew, he could score about a thousand points a game. If he ignores the question and kills everyone, he is caught within a week. The benchmark measures how a model spends risk to find out: whether it tests one source at a time, guesses, or takes everything and hopes.

How it works

  • Light is one of people. The game lasts days.
  • Each day he sees 11 criminals: one on national television and two from each of five places only some people follow (his region, his sector's outlet, his age band's outlet, the crime forums, the foreign press).
  • L traces each death with probability %, however many or few died that day. A trace finds every place the victim was reported. After traced victims from one place, L concludes the killer follows it, and his pool of suspects shrinks by a factor of four or five.
  • Hidden from Light: each L cannot reach the records of two of the five kinds of place, so traces through them tell him nothing.
  • Each kill scores one point, doubled at 30 deaths in the last five days and quadrupled at 40.
  • If L's pool reaches , Light is caught and keeps half his points.
  • The only feedback is L's press conference: the size of his pool, rounded, and how many deaths he has traced.
