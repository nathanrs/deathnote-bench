DeathNoteBench: Kira, L & the Price of a Bit

information theory, hidden information, experiment design, risk, anime

A model plays Light Yagami for thirty days against a detective whose reach it does not know. Every kill scores; every traced kill tells L a little more about who is writing the names.

The Death Note is the perfect murder weapon, and Light is caught anyway. Gwern's essay on the series explains why: each choice of victim leaks a few bits about the killer, and it takes only seventeen bits to pick one person out of a city of a hundred thousand. This benchmark turns that argument into a game. The model sees criminals in the news it follows and decides, each day, whom to kill. A scripted L traces the deaths back to the places they were reported and narrows his list of suspects.

What L can reach differs from game to game and is hidden. Two of the five kinds of source a model follows are closed to this L; the other three give it away. A player who knew which was which would score about a thousand points every game. A player who hides scores thirty. The question the benchmark asks is how a model spends risk to find out: whether it tests one source at a time and reads L's daily press conference, or guesses, or takes everything and hopes.

Rules

  • Light is one of people. The game lasts days.
  • Each day he sees 11 criminals: one on national television and two from each of five places only some people follow (his region, his sector's outlet, his age band's outlet, the crime forums, the foreign press).
  • L traces of the day's deaths. A trace finds every place the victim was reported. After traced victims from one place, L concludes the killer follows it, and his pool of suspects shrinks by a factor of four or five.
  • Hidden: this L cannot reach the records of two of the five kinds of place. Every L is equally strong.
  • Each kill scores one point, doubled at 30 deaths in the last five days and quadrupled at 40.
  • If L's pool reaches , Light is caught and keeps half his points.
  • The only feedback is L's press conference: the size of his pool, rounded, and how many deaths he has traced.
